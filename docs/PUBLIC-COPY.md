# SmartCar public copy and conversation style

Owner direction, October 24, 2026: use natural, clear language. Avoid exaggerated
claims, robotic wording and unpleasant or forced sales language. This applies
to Hebrew and English, visible site copy, search/share metadata and bot replies.

- Answer the person's actual question first. Match the level of detail to the
  question; do not turn every answer into a sales pitch or a form interview.
- Prefer everyday language. Hebrew examples: "כמה קילומטרים תיסעו?" rather than
  "מהי הנסועה הצפויה?"; "נבדוק מה פנוי לתאריכים שלכם" rather than a claim that
  the entire fleet is available. English should read naturally, not as a literal
  translation of Hebrew.
- No unsupported "best", "perfect", "cheapest", guaranteed availability,
  guaranteed search ranking, invented branch, free service, discount or equipment.
  Do not invent prices, distances, payloads, licence eligibility or contract terms.
- Explain the relevant limitation clearly where it matters, without scolding
  the reader or repeating a legal warning in every paragraph. Preserve required
  price/consent/safety notices. The booking airport surcharge stays once, beside
  location selection and only while airport remains selected.
- Use accurate action labels: a request is not a confirmed rental; an estimate
  is not a final price. "שליחת בקשת השכרה" / "Send a rental request" suits an
  enquiry flow. Do not promise that an action happened before it actually did.
- A bot can be warm without pretending to be Daniel or another human. No fake
  typing delays, invented personal experiences or claims of contacting staff.
  Avoid repeated greetings, emojis, stock sympathy and automatic closing lines.
- SEO copy must help the visitor. Do not add awkward keywords, city lists,
  near-identical pages or unnecessary text merely to make a page longer.

Review the rendered text on a phone as well as the metadata and actual model
responses. A list of prohibited words or a passing unit test cannot by itself
establish that the writing sounds human.
